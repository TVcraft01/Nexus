## How the phone was made to hear a sentence

I cannot speak, so the acoustic stimulus was synthesized **on the PC** — a
local neural TTS (piper, `en_US-lessac-medium`) run from `/tmp` — and played
out of the laptop's speakers with `paplay` while the phone's microphone was
open. Nothing about it reaches the app: the phone only ever sees a room with a
voice in it. Piper is a test fixture, not a dependency; nothing was added to
`pubspec.yaml`, and no cloud STT or TTS is involved anywhere in this run.

The laptop's own microphone, recorded while `paplay` ran, measured the phrase
at `mean -18.7 dB / max 0.0 dB`, so the room really did get the sound.

- **The recognizer was the on-device one** — the path with no network in it at

- **The transcript is what was said**: "What time is it", from audio that
  travelled laptop speaker → room → phone microphone. Nothing was typed.
- **The reply was handed to the system speech engine** and the exchange ran
  through the ordinary pipeline (the answer card was "It's 19:47").
- The listening state on screen, the pill wearing the accent ring with the stop
  control where the mic was: [shots-20261010/10-voice-listening-ring.png](shots-20261010/10-voice-listening-ring.png)

A first attempt is worth recording too: with the phrase played at 100% sink
volume, the recognizer answered

and a real transcript. That peak field was added for exactly this reason: a
silent room and a dead microphone are the same `null` without it.

## Update — 2026-10-11, 01:00: the fetch, on the phone (silent run)

A second device run closed most of the list above. One rule changed
mid-run: **the room had to be silent** (no audio), so the fetch sentence
was never spoken into the phone as live speech. What did run, and what it
showed:

### The rig had to be rebuilt — and one real bug surfaced

Both rehearsal peers and the phone had gone down since the earlier run.
The phone came back with its pairings, and re-paired over the tunnel
(`pairWith -> ok` for `peer-pc-a` and `peer-pc-b`). Then every ping/pong
still arrived — and every one was **unproven** (`pong <- peer-pc-a
(unproven)`), no supervisor ever said `is up again`, and an encrypted
Files browse against the peer timed out.

Root cause: `_handlePairRequest` / `pairWith` store the new pairing secret
but never clear `MeshService._sessionKeys[peer.id]`. The long-running peer
had cached the session key from the previous pairing and kept signing and
verifying with it: presence tags never verified (unproven both ways), and
encrypted frames could not be decrypted (the browse timeout). Restarting
the peers rebuilt the cache from the newly stored secret, and everything
proved at once:

```
NEXUS mesh: pong <- peer-pc-a             (no "(unproven)")
NexusSupervisor: peer peer-pc-a is up again (0s of silence before it answered)
```

Devices then read **Online** with "2 of 4 devices reachable right now"
(`shots-20261011/01-devices-online.png`). The bug is real and fixable in
one line on both sides: re-pairing must drop the cached session key.

### STT, on-device, from real speech

Before the silence rule, the laptop speakers put "What time is it" into
the room once, and the phone's **on-device recognizer** wrote it down:

```
speech: listening on-device (no network)
RecognitionClient: #onResults withSpeech: true
speech: heard "What time is it" (peak 10.0dB)
```

The fetch sentence itself was tried from the speakers at 01:00 and came
back `nothing recognized (error 7)` — which turned out to be a **muted
sink on the PC** (`paplay` was writing into `Mute: yes`; sweep after sweep
of "too quiet / too loud" was sound that never left the machine). After
unmuting, "What time is it" transcribed on the first try. The fetch
sentence was never re-spoken, because the room went silent right after.
